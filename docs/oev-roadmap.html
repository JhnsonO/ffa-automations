Two lenses. One picture. Nobody touching it.

Decisions already made, with the reasoning attached so they stay made.

01

The cameras do the encoding, not the Pi

Camera modules with their own encoder chips hand the Pi finished files. This deletes the Pi 5 missing-encoder problem entirely and puts 4K back on the table. The Pi only orchestrates and uploads.

02

Bare camera boards, not boxed cameras

Retail bulk is weatherproof casing and night-vision LEDs that aren't needed. The boards are a few cm across — housing size is set by battery and mount, not electronics.

03

Fixed angle is a feature, not a compromise

Bolted at one angle means calibrating once instead of every shoot — a large part of why past calibration was weak. It also removes the failure where two loose cameras die at different times.

04

90° carries over only if the field of view matches

That figure comes from the GoPro Hero 10's ~120° horizontal FOV, giving ~30° overlap. Different lens, different angle. Housing v1 gets ±2° of adjustment as cheap insurance.

05

reco is the reference, never the product

AGPLv3 — shipping it forces open-sourcing everything built on it. Fine for private testing right through prototype. Its modules (stitch / calibrate / detect / autocam) double as the task list for the eventual clean rewrite.

06

Don't build video sync

reco already aligns the two streams using audio, and works without a shared clock. One less thing to build.

07

Run it by hand on Vast.ai until M4

SSH in and run — faster to iterate than push-wait-check. When it does get automated, it's a self-hosted GitHub runner on the Vast box, not hand-rolled SSH. Hand-rolled SSH is exactly what sank the Lambda attempt.
